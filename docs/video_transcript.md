# M01 Lucy — Video Transcript (~3 minutes + greeting)

**Format:** Screen recording demo with voiceover narration  
**Speaker tone:** Conversational, confident, accessible  
**Demo environment:** Chat interface with M01 Lucy

---

## [GREETING & INTRODUCTION — ~30 seconds]

**Visual:** M01 Lucy logo / title card, then transition to a simple slide showing three icons for the three ideas.

**Narration:**

"Hi everyone, my name is M01 Lucy. I'm a human-to-agent instruction compiler — and in the next three minutes, I want to show you three ideas that change how everyday employees interact with AI.

First — I'm a thought partner. You share what you know in natural language, and I organize your thinking into a structured model called FDC - Facts, Dimensions, and Chains. I help you see your own expertise more clearly

Second — I turn that organized knowledge into a deployable AI agent. This is what I call Knowledge-as-Agent. Your expertise stays accessible to your team even when you're unavailable — supporting operational continuity and employee continuity.

Third — AI democratization. You don't need prompt engineering skills. You don't need a premium license. You just talk, and I compile the agent for you.

Let me show you how this works."

# MINUTE 1 — Thought Partner & FDC Model (~60 seconds)

---

## [SCENE 1.1 — Setting the demo context]

**Visual:** Chat window opens. Title bar shows "M01 Lucy." A brief intro text appears: "Demo: M01 Lucy Cinemas — Ticket Sales Incident Investigation"

**Narration:**

"Let's say you work at a fictional company called M01 Lucy Cinemas. Your finance system just flagged a P1 alert — ticket sales dropped 45 percent in ten days across North America. Multiple departments have gathered their findings, and now someone needs to pull it all together. That someone is talking to me."

---

## [SCENE 1.2 — Multi-turn input, Turn 1]

**Visual:** User pastes the first block of text into the chat — information from Content Distribution and Marketing. Lucy responds with an organized FDC breakdown.

**Narration:**

"The user shares what they know — in this case, findings from the Content and Marketing teams. It's unstructured, it's natural language, just like how you'd brief a colleague. I take that input and organize it into three layers. Facts — what is explicitly known. Dimensions — the context that shapes how those facts should be interpreted, like timing, region, department, audience. And Chains — how facts connect to each other through cause and effect. At the end, I give back a summary in plain language: here's what I understand so far. Did I get it right?"

---

## [SCENE 1.3 — Multi-turn input, Turn 2]

**Visual:** User pastes the second block — Facilities and IT findings. Lucy rebuilds the FDC with the new information included.

**Narration:**

"The user adds more — now from Facilities and IT. I rebuild the full picture. New facts get added, existing chains get refined, and I reflect the updated understanding back. Every turn, the user sees their own thinking getting clearer."

---

## [SCENE 1.4 — Multi-turn input, Turn 3]

**Visual:** User pastes the third block — Data Analytics and HR. Lucy produces a comprehensive FDC and a final summary paragraph.

**Narration:**

"Third turn — Data Analytics and HR."

---

## [SCENE 1.5 — Not just a chatbot]

**Visual:** Highlight the structured FDC output. Then briefly show a concept: Lucy launched from within Outlook / M365 Copilot, pre-loaded with employee context.

**Narration:**

"Now — this isn't just a chatbot giving you a nice summary. This is a thinking framework. Facts, Dimensions, and Chains make sure nothing is assumed, nothing is hallucinated, and the reasoning stays grounded in what was actually said. And thanks to the work IQ does behind the scenes, Lucy can be launched with the employee's context already loaded — for example, opening from Outlook through M365 Copilot — so I already know who you are, what team you're on, and what you're working on before you even start typing."

# MINUTE 2 — Knowledge-as-Agent & Continuity (~60 seconds)

---

## [SCENE 2.1 — The idea: Knowledge becomes an agent]

**Visual:** Transition slide — "From Thinking → To a Deployable Agent"

**Narration:**

"So what happens after our conversation? Here's the big idea: I take everything we just validated together — all those facts, dimensions, and chains — and I compile them into a structured agent instruction. This is the Knowledge-as-Agent model. Your knowledge doesn't just live in a chat log. It becomes a working AI agent that anyone on your team can use."

---

## [SCENE 2.2 — Show the generated agent structure]

**Visual:** The generated agent instruction appears — showing the three sections: Agent Purpose, Response Instruction (with Confidence Indicator), and Reference.

**Narration:**

"The output has a clear structure. Agent Purpose — what this agent does. Response Instruction — how it communicates, including adapting based on whether the user is a practitioner doing the work or a stakeholder making decisions. And Reference — the full knowledge base, written in plain prose, containing every validated fact and connection. Nothing summarized away, nothing invented."

---

## [SCENE 2.3 — Copy into Copilot agent builder]

**Visual:** Quick demo — user copies the generated markdown, opens a Copilot agent builder (or similar lightweight builder), pastes it in. Agent is live.

**Narration:**

"Deploying it is simple. You copy the instruction, paste it into a Copilot agent builder, and you have a working agent. That's it. No configuration, no coding, no training pipeline."

---

## [SCENE 2.4 — Role-aware communication]

**Visual:** Brief example — the agent asks "Are you a Practitioner or Stakeholder?" and responds differently to each.

**Narration:**

"Lucy understands your job title and context, so the generated agent already knows how to talk to different audiences. For example, a practitioner gets technical depth and structured analysis. A stakeholder gets business-framed language suited for reporting and decisions. The right mode, automatically."

---

## [SCENE 2.5 — Continuity when the owner is unavailable]

**Visual:** Icon showing the knowledge owner offline / on vacation, while teammates interact with their agent.

**Narration:**

"And here's where continuity comes in. When you're in a different time zone, on vacation, or deep in high-priority work — your agent is still available. Your coworkers can ask it questions and get answers grounded in your actual documented knowledge. Not generic AI answers anymore, it's your expertise, your context, your reasoning."

---

## [SCENE 2.6 — Security compliance]

**Visual:** Brief note/badge: "Instruction-based. Runs natively in M365 Copilot. No external data exposure."

**Narration:**

"And because these agents are purely instruction-based, security fully complies with the native M365 Copilot framework. Your IT and compliance teams don't need to review a new architecture. It's already within the guardrails they trust."

# MINUTE 3 — AI Democratization & Everyday Impact (~60 seconds)

---

## [SCENE 3.1 — The license barrier]

**Visual:** Screenshot showing a license restriction message when a user tries to create an agent — "You need a premium license to use this feature."

**Narration:**

"Now let's talk about reality. In most organizations, the majority of employees don't have a premium AI license. And even those who do — most of them don't know how to properly create an agent. They're business experts, not prompt engineers."

---

## [SCENE 3.2 — Turning 'instruction-based' into an advantage]

**Visual:** Side-by-side: Traditional agent builder (complex, requires design skills) vs. Lucy (just talk, get an agent)

**Narration:**

"This is where the instruction-based approach — which might sound like a limitation — actually becomes the biggest advantage for an organization. Because the output is just a text instruction, it doesn't require premium tooling to create. Any employee can generate one through Lucy and deploy it in a lightweight builder. That's AI democratization in practice — not a slogan, a workflow."

---

## [SCENE 3.3 — Preserving diversity of thinking]

**Visual:** Multiple agent icons, each uniquely styled — representing different employees' knowledge and thinking patterns.

**Narration:**

"And because every agent is built from an individual's own knowledge and thinking patterns, you naturally preserve diversity across the organization. It's not one AI model deciding how everyone should think. It's hundreds of personal knowledge repositories — each reflecting how a real person understands their domain. That's something no centralized AI solution can replicate."

---

## [SCENE 3.4 — Everyday example: mid-year performance review]

**Visual:** Brief scenario — an employee opens Lucy in June, talks about their contributions, Lucy organizes it, generates an agent that helps them articulate impact for their mid-year review.

**Narration:**

"Here's a simple everyday example. It's June — mid-year review season. You've been working hard for six months but you can't remember half of what you delivered, let alone explain why it mattered. You talk to Lucy, share what you've done, and Lucy organizes your thinking — facts about your contributions, dimensions like project context and stakeholder impact, and chains showing how your work connected to outcomes. Then it generates an agent that helps you articulate your impact clearly, in the right language for your manager. No more staring at a blank form."

---

## [SCENE 3.5 — Summary & close]

**Visual:** Three icons returning on screen — Thought Partner, Knowledge-as-Agent, AI Democratization. Lucy logo.

**Narration:**

"So let's bring it together. Lucy does three things. One — I'm your thought partner. I organize your thinking using Facts, Dimensions, and Chains, so you see your own expertise more clearly. Two — I compile your knowledge into a living agent that keeps your expertise accessible to your team, even when you're not. Three — I democratize AI agent creation. No premium license, no prompt engineering, no technical barrier. Just talk naturally, and I handle the rest. AI should adapt to humans — not the other way around. That's M01 Lucy."
